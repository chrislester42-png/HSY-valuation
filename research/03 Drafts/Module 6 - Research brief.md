## Our company and our call

The Hershey Company (NYSE: HSY), the US chocolate and confectionery maker. Our valuation (DCF, cross-checked with peer multiples) puts it at $189 to $218 a share against a $160.19 price, so our call is **Buy below $189, avoid above $218**; the call rests on operating margin recovering from 12.3% in FY2025 as cheaper cocoa reaches the income statement.

## What our numbers assume (context, not facts to research)

These are our own forecast and valuation inputs, so you can say which risk or event would move which one. Do not search for them.

- Revenue growth: 5.2% in FY2026, then 2.5% and 2.6% in FY2027 and FY2028 (analyst consensus). Bear case: 1.5 points lower every year.
- EBITDA margin: 24.7% in FY2026, 26.9% in FY2027, 27.5% in FY2028, up from 16.6% in FY2025. Bear case: 3 points lower every year.
- WACC 6.78%, built on a 5.28% 10-year Treasury yield and a beta of 0.41. Bear case: 7.56%.
- Perpetual growth 3.0%. Bear case: 2.5%. About nine-tenths of our enterprise value sits in the terminal value, beyond 2028.
- Management guided FY2026 net sales growth of 4.5% to 5.0% and about 400 basis points of margin improvement (Q2 2026). In Q2 2026, confectionery volume fell about 10% as prices rose about 14%.

## Question 1: the risks

What are the four or five risks that could most hurt our call or our valuation over the next twelve months? Answer each with dated figures. Start from these five; replace one if the company's own risk factors or the latest earnings call point to something more serious, and say why.

1. **Cocoa and the margin recovery.** Where cocoa futures stand now against their late-2024 peak, how much of Hershey's cost is hedged and for how long, and what management has said about when lower cocoa costs reach gross margin. Anything that could push cocoa back up (West African crop forecasts, weather, disease, export or farmgate price changes).
2. **Volume and price elasticity.** How far volume has fallen as prices rose, by quarter; retailer or consumer pushback; promotion levels; share loss to private label or rivals. Include any company or industry data on weight-loss drugs (GLP-1) and snack demand, and any state limits on buying candy with SNAP benefits, if they apply to Hershey.
3. **Tariffs and trade.** US tariffs or trade actions on cocoa, sugar, or other inputs Hershey imports, with rates and effective dates, and what the company has said they cost.
4. **Rates and the cost of capital.** The current 10-year Treasury yield against our 5.28%, and any change to Hershey's credit rating, debt, or interest cost. Our value is very sensitive to the gap between WACC and growth.
5. **Other company-specific risks** named in the latest 10-K or 10-Q: litigation, product recalls, regulation of ingredients or labels (for example synthetic food dyes), supply chain, or leadership changes.

For each risk, also name the **published number that would show it is happening** (for example a cocoa futures price, a quarterly volume change, a gross margin figure) and when that number is next published.

## Question 2: the dated events

What dated events in the next twelve months (from the date you run this) could move Hershey's value? Look for:

- Earnings dates and what each will report: Q3 2026, Q4 and full-year 2026 (with FY2027 guidance), Q1 2027, Q2 2027.
- Guidance updates, investor days, or industry conference presentations.
- Product launches or major seasons that matter to results (Halloween, Valentine's Day, Easter), only where the company or its filings say they matter.
- Regulatory or legal decisions with a date: court rulings, tariff decisions, ingredient or labeling rules, SNAP waiver start dates.
- Contracts, acquisitions, divestitures, or debt maturities with a date.
- Input cost events: West African main-crop and mid-crop timing, farmgate price announcements, ICCO crop forecasts.

**When a date is not announced yet:** give the expected month marked "expected", and give last year's date for the same event as the evidence (for example: "Q4 2026 results: February 2027, expected. Last year's Q4 results came out on [date], [source]."). Never invent a date.

## Sources to prefer

1. The company's own filings with the SEC: the latest Form 10-K and Form 10-Q, especially Risk Factors and Management's Discussion and Analysis (MD&A).
